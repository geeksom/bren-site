Before writing any copy, answer three questions:

1. **Who is it for?** → IC, People Manager, Senior Leader, or Executive Buyer (Section 2)
2. **What page/channel?** → Find the playbook (Section 6)
3. **What stage?** → Cold (never heard of us), Warm (evaluating / on the site), Customer (onboarded)

Everything else is guidance with examples. Facts (prices, limits, integrations, security) come from the numbered knowledge docs and override anything implied here.

## 1. Positioning Foundation (strategic core — not for external use verbatim)

### The bet

Knowledge-work companies run on status: stand-ups, syncs, "quick updates", catch-up calls, onboarding decks, TL;DR threads nobody reads. All of it exists because information about *what is happening* is scattered across chat, meetings, docs, tickets and people's heads. Bren bets that if a system already reads all of that — with the same permissions people have — then **every person can have a personal assistant that keeps them posted and answers any question about work**, and most status rituals can simply stop.

- **Market:** knowledge-work organisations of 50–2,000 people, remote/hybrid, already on Slack or Teams and a Jira/Notion-class project tool
- **Use case:** kill status overhead, keep everyone (ICs → C-level) informed at their altitude, and surface where to intervene before it becomes a fire
- **Differentiation in plain language:** project tools show tasks, not momentum. Meeting note-takers capture one meeting, not the company. Generic AI chat knows the internet, not your org. Bren is the only product built on the *company graph* — people, teams, projects, objectives, time — with proactive, persona-specific briefs, not just a search box.

### The category

**AI chief of staff for every person in the company.**

"Personal assistant / PA" is the *felt* metaphor ("Everyone gets a PA now") — it describes what it feels like to have Bren. "Chief of staff" is the *job* metaphor for leaders. The category word we own is **chief of staff**; we do not compete in "project management", "meeting notes" or "enterprise search" — those are inputs to Bren, not peers.

### What we are replacing (tool/ritual displacement)

| We displace | The wedge |
|---|---|
| **Daily stand-ups and weekly status meetings** (most common) | "Fifteen people × 20 minutes × every day. Bren gives the manager the same picture from 100-word async updates, and gives everyone the 20 minutes back." |
| **"What's the latest on X?" pings** | "Ask Bren. Cited answer in seconds. Nobody interrupted." |
| **Onboarding decks / Notion wikis that go stale** | "Bren's Second Brain is the company's chronological memory. It's never out of date because it's built from what actually happened." |
| **Leadership catch-up calls to 'get a feel'** | "Leaders get Monday Morning Brief and Leadership Pulse. They know where to intervene before 9am." |
| **Meeting note-takers** (Otter, Fireflies, Gong-lite) | "They summarise a meeting. Bren summarises the company. Meetings are one of six streams." |
| **Generic AI chat / Copilot** | "It knows the internet and your docs. It doesn't know who owns what, what's slipping, or who's on leave." |

### What we are NOT (guardrails for writers)

Bren is **not**:

- **A project-management tool.** We don't replace Jira, Notion or ClickUp. We read from them and write summaries back.
- **A meeting bot.** Bren never joins calls. It consumes transcripts your meeting tool already produces.
- **Employee surveillance.** No keystrokes, screens, location, app-usage tracking. Copy that sounds like "monitor productivity" is off-brand — rewrite as "keep everyone posted".
- **A generic chatbot for docs.** Ask Bren is the door, the company graph is the house.
- **An HR/performance system.** Managers can mark wins and losses and keep private notes; Bren does not score people or replace reviews.
- **Enterprise-only.** Self-serve sign-up, 14-day trial, transparent pricing. Enterprise is a plan, not the only way in.

### The one-line pitch

> **Bren is the AI chief of staff for every person in your company. It connects to your chat, meetings, docs and project tools, builds a living memory of the organisation, and keeps everyone posted — from individual contributors to the CEO — while answering any question about work in seconds.**

**Usage notes:**
- Full version: Platform page, about sections, demo decks, press.
- Short version (space-constrained): *"Bren keeps your whole company posted and answers any question about work — so you can stop being busy and be productive."*
- Ultra-short (bios, footers): *"Everyone gets a PA now."*
- Never open cold outbound with the pitch. Cold copy opens with the ritual the prospect hates (the stand-up, the sync, the "quick question").

### The enemy

**Status overhead.** The stand-up, the sync, the "quick update", the Friday report nobody reads, the onboarding deck from last quarter. This is the enemy in all copy — not a competitor brand. It works for every persona and never ages.

Unifying message across all personas: **you are busy staying informed; you should be busy doing the work.**

## 2. The Buyer

Bren has *users* at every level and *buyers* at two. Copy usually addresses a user's pain and closes on a buyer's outcome.

### Primary persona 1: The People Manager (user + champion)

**Role & context:** Manages 2–4 teams or 6–25 people at a 50–2,000 person company. Engineering manager, product lead, head of a function. Often the person who brings Bren in.

**Day-to-day reality:** Spends a significant share of the week delegating and staying updated. Runs the stand-up, then still has to chase. Reads three tools to answer one question from their own manager. Writes the weekly update on Friday at 5pm from memory.

**What they believe before they find us:**
- "Stand-ups are annoying but necessary — how else do I know what's happening?" (Bren's async standup + daily digest is the *how else*.)
- "Async updates die after two weeks." (True without a consumer. Bren is the consumer: updates feed digests, timelines and red flags, so they have a point.)
- "Another tool means more overhead for my team." (One ~100-word update a day, in Slack, replaces the stand-up. Net time goes down.)

**The job they're hiring for:** *"Know what's happening on my teams without running the meeting or chasing people."*

**Message angle:** practical, time-math heavy, peer-to-peer. Lead with the stand-up. Show the daily digest and red flags. Prove the update is smaller than the meeting.

**Buys:** self-serve for their team (Team plan) or champions a Business trial; needs their team to actually like it.

### Primary persona 2: The Senior Leader (Director / VP / C-level — user + economic buyer)

**Role & context:** Director, VP, COO, CEO, Chief of Staff at a 100–2,000 person company. Owns outcomes across multiple teams. Can't stay updated without long, frequent meetings or interrogating reportees.

**Day-to-day reality:** Constantly vigilant — putting out fires or trying to understand business momentum. Decisions queue behind their calendar. Learns about problems two weeks late, in a meeting, from someone reluctant to say it.

**What they believe before they find us:**
- "My dashboards tell me status." (They show ticket counts; they don't show *momentum*, risk or who's overloaded.)
- "I'd need a chief of staff for this, and I can't justify the headcount." (Exactly — Bren is the chief of staff at $899/mo for 100 people.)
- "AI summaries will miss the nuance." (Every line cites its source; expand any segment; nothing is invented.)
- "Security/IT will block it." (SOC 2 Type II, permission mirroring, no training on customer data, SSO — see `06-security-compliance.md`.)

**The job they're hiring for:** *"See where to intervene before it becomes a fire — without adding meetings."*

**Message angle:** altitude, momentum, intervention. Monday Morning Brief and Leadership Pulse are the hero visuals. Quantify meeting time reclaimed across the org.

**Buys:** via demo (Business or Enterprise), after security review and a pilot on one department.

### Persona 3: The Individual Contributor (user — must love it, never blocks it)

**Role & context:** Engineer, designer, marketer, analyst. Interrupted all day by "quick questions"; loses an afternoon to context-switching after every long meeting; comes back from leave to 400 unread messages.

**Message angle:** protection and clarity. "Post one update. Never be asked 'what's the status?' again." Ask Bren instead of asking a colleague. Catch up after leave in ten minutes. Private notes stay private.

**Never:** frame Bren as reporting on ICs to their manager. Frame it as ICs owning their narrative.

### Persona 4: The New Hire / Returner (moment-based)

Not a buyer, but the most emotionally resonant story. "Productive in five days, not five weeks." Use in Second Brain copy, onboarding sections, testimonials.

### Secondary stakeholders

- **IT / Security** (approves the connection): cares about scopes, data residency, training policy, SSO/SCIM. Copy artifact: the Security & Trust section and `06-security-compliance.md`. Never make them the buyer; make them comfortable.
- **HR / People** (worries about surveillance): message: "Bren summarises what people choose to post and what's already in work tools. No monitoring. Private notes are private, even from admins."
- **Finance / CFO** (approves Business/Enterprise): cares about flat pricing and payback. Message: "$899/mo for 100 people is less than one hour of their combined time per month."

## 3. The Problem Frame

### The approved problem statement

> **Your people spend their days staying informed instead of doing the work. Stand-ups, syncs, status threads, catch-up calls — all of it exists because nobody can see what's actually happening. Meanwhile leaders find out about problems two weeks late.**

### The three layers (use in order — each escalates)

1. **Time drain.** Daily stand-ups, weekly syncs, "quick questions", Friday reports. Multiply by headcount. This is the visible, quantifiable cost.
2. **Momentum blindness.** Managers of 2–4 teams and senior leaders cannot stay updated without meetings or interrogating reportees. Decisions wait for calendars. Fires are discovered, not predicted. *This is the emotional core for leaders: flying blind while looking busy.*
3. **Context loss.** Onboarding takes weeks; returning from leave takes days; when someone leaves, their context leaves with them. The company has no memory — only people who happen to remember.

### Emotional vs. rational framing — when to use each

| | Emotional framing | Rational framing |
|---|---|---|
| **The move** | Make the ritual feel absurd, or the blindness feel dangerous | Make the time/money math undeniable |
| **Sounds like** | "You found out the launch was slipping in a meeting, from someone who'd known for a week." / "Your new hire's third week: still asking where things are." | "12 people × 20-minute stand-up × 250 days = 1,000 hours a year — for one team." / "$899/month for 100 people is under $9 per person." |
| **Use for** | Hero headlines, cold openers, feature-page intros | Below the fold, pricing page, ROI sections, FAQ |
| **Persona lean** | Senior Leader (blindness), IC (interruption) | People Manager (time math), CFO (payback) |

**Rule:** open emotional, close rational. One hook, then specifics.

## 4. The Differentiation Story

Four supporting arguments, in order. Lead with #1 for cold audiences; #4 closes evaluations with security-minded buyers.

### Argument 1: It keeps you posted — you don't have to ask

**Claim:** Bren pushes the right brief to the right person at the right time: daily digests for leads, weekly for managers and ICs, Monday Morning Brief and Leadership Pulse for leaders, monthly recaps for everyone.
**So what:** Status meetings become optional. Nobody writes the Friday report.
**Proof points / feature callouts:** AI Digests, Monday Morning Brief, Leadership Pulse, Red-Flag Detection, Year in Review.

### Argument 2: It knows the whole company, not just the docs

**Claim:** Bren is built on the company graph — teams, hierarchy, projects, tasks, OKRs, time — fed by chat, meetings, docs, project tools and code.
**So what:** It can answer "who owns the dependency that's slipping?" and "are we on track for Q3?" — questions a doc search can't.
**Proof points:** 19 integrations; Hierarchical Work Timeline; OKR tracking; Second Brain; Ask Bren in Slack/Teams.

### Argument 3: Every answer is cited, nothing is invented

**Claim:** Every line in a digest or Ask Bren answer links to the message, ticket, doc or meeting it came from. Missing updates are flagged, never fabricated.
**So what:** Leaders can trust it and drill in; ICs aren't misrepresented.
**Proof points:** source citations, "elaborate this" drill-down, clarification requests, private notes excluded by default.

### Argument 4: Permission-aware by design

**Claim:** Bren mirrors permissions from every source tool and adds Participatory / Public / Selective visibility on top. Nobody sees anything in Bren they couldn't see in the source.
**So what:** Security and HR say yes. Roll it out company-wide without a visibility audit.
**Proof points:** SOC 2 Type II, permission mirroring, no training on customer data, SAML SSO (Business), SCIM + data residency + VPC (Enterprise).

### Claims logic: can / can't / shouldn't

**CAN claim (fictional but consistent — see numbered docs):**
- Prices: $299 Team (25 seats), $899 Business (100 seats), Enterprise custom
- 14-day free trial, no credit card, Business features
- 19 integrations across chat, meetings, docs, project management, code
- SOC 2 Type II; GDPR/CCPA; AES-256 at rest; TLS 1.3; no training on customer data
- First digest within 24 hours of connecting Slack/Teams + one PM tool
- Promise-level outcomes (use these exact numbers): **"Cut status meetings by 70%"**, **"New hires productive in 5 days, not 5 weeks"**, **"Leaders know where to intervene before Monday 9am"**, **"1,000 hours a year reclaimed per 12-person team"**
- Fictional customer proof from the approved roster: Northwind Robotics, Larkspur Health, Orbital Freight, Halvard & Finch, Tessel Systems, Meridian Learning (see `02-positioning.md` for quotes)

**CAN'T claim (not in the product today):**
- Voice updates, Chrome extension, 2-way doc sync, automated KR metrics, AI-worker tracking → "on the roadmap"
- Meeting recording/joining as a bot
- Native mobile app
- Public API on Team/Business
- Any integration not in the list of 19 (Asana, Linear, Salesforce etc. → "on the roadmap")
- Specific uptime figures except the Enterprise 99.9% SLA

**SHOULDN'T claim (true-ish but strategically wrong):**
- "Replaces your managers" / "replaces 1:1s" — Bren removes status overhead, not leadership
- "Monitor employee productivity" — surveillance frame; say "keep everyone posted"
- "Replaces Jira/Notion" — we sit on top
- "Better than ChatGPT" — never anchor to general-purpose AI
- Naming real companies as customers or competitors in a disparaging way

### Rules of engagement for competitor mentions

- Named competitor comparisons are not on this site. When a prospect names one (Otter, Fireflies, Notion AI, Copilot, Glean, Asana Intelligence), agree with what it's good at, then reframe to the company graph and proactive briefs.
- Never say a competitor is "legacy" or "dead". Punch at the category limitation: "it summarises a meeting, not a company".

## 5. Voice & Tone

**The voice: calm operator.** A chief of staff who has run a 300-person org talking to peers — not a vendor talking to leads. Confident, plain, slightly wry about the rituals everyone secretly hates.

Four properties:

1. **Specific over clever.** "1,000 hours a year for a 12-person team" beats "reclaim your time". Name the ritual, the moment, the number.
2. **Altitude-aware.** Copy for an IC talks about interruptions; for a VP, about momentum and intervention. Never mix altitudes in one paragraph.
3. **Bold, not hype.** Big promises are allowed ("cut status meetings by 70%") because the number does the work. Hype adjectives are not.
4. **Human, not HR.** We say "people", "your team", "the CEO". Never "resources", "headcount optimisation", "workforce visibility".

### Voice do / don't

| Don't write | Write instead | Why |
|---|---|---|
| "Revolutionise team collaboration with AI-powered insights" | "Cancel the stand-up. Bren already knows." | Hype words are skimmed; the plain claim is bigger |
| "Gain visibility into workforce productivity" | "Know what's happening on all four of your teams before your coffee's cold." | Surveillance frame vs. a moment the manager recognises |
| "Leverage our advanced LLM technology" | "Every line links to the message, ticket or doc it came from." | Buyers don't buy LLMs; they buy trust |
| "Empower every employee" | "Everyone gets a PA now." | Tagline beats abstraction |
| "Affordable for teams of every size" | "$299/month for up to 25 people. Try it free for 14 days." | Price is a fact, not an adjective |
| "Never miss an update again!!" | "You found out in a meeting. Bren would have told you Monday." | Urgency through specificity, not punctuation |

### Tone by context

- **Website hero:** bold, short, slightly provocative about rituals. One promise per headline.
- **Feature pages:** concrete and mechanistic. Problem → how Bren does it → who it's for → facts.
- **Pricing/FAQ:** plain, generous, precise. Numbers and rules, no persuasion words.
- **Legal:** standard, readable, complete.
- **In-product / assistant:** warm, brief, helpful. Never robotic-formal ("How may I assist you today?" banned), never fake-casual ("Hey there! 👋" banned).
- **Objection responses:** calm, generous to the alternative, precise about the difference.

## 6. Messaging by Page/Channel

### Headline patterns that work for this positioning

1. **The ritual pattern:** name the ritual, then make it optional. — *"Cancel the stand-up. Bren already knows."*
2. **The altitude pattern:** everyone informed at their level. — *"Everyone gets a PA now."* / *"The chief of staff every leader wanted. Now every person has one."*
3. **The blindness pattern:** leaders finding out late. — *"Know where to intervene before Monday 9am."*
4. **The memory pattern:** context that doesn't leave. — *"Your company finally has a memory."*
5. **The time-math pattern:** — *"Give every team 1,000 hours back this year."*

Avoid: question headlines ("Tired of status meetings?"), "AI-powered" as the lead noun, "collaboration", and any headline that would work for Asana or Notion.

### 6a. Home page

- **Structure:** H1 (ritual or altitude pattern) → sub (one-line pitch, short version) → two CTAs → proof bar → fictional logo bar → three pillars (Arguments 1, 2, 3) → persona tabs (IC / Manager / Leadership) → how it works (connect → memory → briefs) → integrations strip → testimonials (fictional) → pricing teaser → FAQ (8–10) → CTA band.
- **Approved H1/sub pair:** *"Everyone gets a PA now."* / *"Bren connects to your chat, meetings, docs and project tools, keeps every person posted at their level, and answers any question about work in seconds. Stop being busy. Be productive."*
- **Alternate H1 (A/B):** *"Cancel the stand-up. Bren already knows."*
- **Proof bar:** `19 integrations · first brief within 24 hours · SOC 2 Type II · 14-day free trial, no card`
- **CTA convention:** primary = **"Start free trial"** (→ /signup/), secondary = **"Book a demo"** (→ /book-demo/). Both appear in the hero and the closing band. Never "Learn more" as a primary.

### 6b. Platform page

- Explain the pipeline (data streams → company graph → insights → alerts) with the persona lens.
- Include Security & Trust as a full section (SOC 2, encryption, permission mirroring, training policy, SSO/SCIM/residency by plan).
- Close with the permissions model explained plainly (Participatory / Public / Selective).

### 6c. Feature pages (15)

- **Template:** hero (H1 + one-liner + both CTAs) → "The problem" (one ritual or moment, emotional) → "How Bren does it" (3–4 mechanistic blocks) → "Who it's for" (altitude) → mini-FAQ (3–4 Qs with concrete facts incl. plan availability) → related features → CTA band.
- Each page names its **plan availability** explicitly (e.g. "Available on Business and Enterprise").
- Each page has one hero visual: an HTML/CSS mock of the feature (chat window, digest card, timeline, brief) — no stock imagery.

### 6d. Pricing page

- Three cards, Business highlighted ("Most popular"). Monthly/annual toggle (annual = 2 months free).
- Feature comparison table below cards. Seat overage stated plainly. Enterprise = "Talk to us" → /book-demo/.
- FAQ of 10–12 items covering trial, seats, overage, annual, upgrade/downgrade, cancellation, data deletion, discounts, invoicing, security, SSO, what Enterprise adds.
- Voice: plain and generous. No urgency, no "limited time".

### 6e. Integrations page

- Grouped by category with direction (2-way / incoming). Each integration: one-line "what Bren reads" + "what Bren writes" + plan availability.
- Roadmap integrations listed separately and labelled.

### 6f. Sign-up and Book-a-demo pages

- **/signup/**: H1 *"Start your 14-day free trial"*, sub: "No credit card. Full Business features. Connect Slack or Teams and see your first digest within 24 hours." Fields: name, work email, company, team size, project tool. Thank-you: "Check your inbox — your workspace link is on its way." Include a small "Prefer a walkthrough? Book a demo" link.
- **/book-demo/**: H1 *"See Bren on your org, not ours"*, sub: "30 minutes with a product specialist. We'll map your teams, tools and rollout — and give you a pilot plan." Fields: name, work email, company, seats, tools in use, use case, timeframe. Thank-you: "We'll confirm a time within one business day." Include "Want to try it first? Start free trial."

### 6g. Assistant / widget copy (Konvars on this site)

- **Greeting (admin-editable):** *"Hi — I know Bren inside-out. What are you trying to fix: stand-ups, staying informed, or onboarding?"*
- **Steering rule:** ≤100 seats, wants to try, no procurement questions → Start free trial. 100+ seats, SSO/SCIM/residency/security review, multi-team rollout, budget cycle questions → Book a demo. See `10-conversion-playbook.md`.
- **Fallback:** *"I don't want to guess on that. Leave your email and someone from the Bren team will answer today."*

## 7. Objection Handling

Format: objection → approved response logic → example line. Agree first, then reframe. Never get defensive. Extended Q&A lives in `09-objections-faq.md`.

**"We already do async stand-ups in Slack / Geekbot."**
→ Agree, name the missing consumer. *"Great — keep the habit. The problem with async updates is nobody reads them after week two. Bren is the reader: updates become daily digests, timelines and red flags, so posting one actually pays off."*

**"AI summaries will miss nuance or get things wrong."**
→ Lead with citations and drill-down. *"Every line links to the message, ticket or doc it came from, and you can expand any segment. If an update is missing, Bren says it's missing — it never invents one."*

**"This feels like surveillance."**
→ Agree that surveillance would be bad; draw the line. *"Bren only reads work tools people already share and the updates they choose to post. No keystrokes, screens or app tracking. Private notes are invisible even to admins, and peers never get performance insights about each other."*

**"Our data is sensitive — where does it go, is it used for training?"**
→ Facts. *"Encrypted at rest and in transit, SOC 2 Type II, never used to train models. Business adds SAML SSO; Enterprise adds SCIM, EU/US data residency, custom retention and a private-VPC option. Happy to send the security overview."*

**"We have Notion AI / Copilot / Glean — isn't that the same?"**
→ Agree on docs, reframe to graph and proactivity. *"For searching docs, yes. Bren also knows who owns what, what's slipping, who's on leave and how it ties to your OKRs — and it tells you before you ask."*

**"$899 a month is a lot for summaries."**
→ Reject the denominator. *"For 100 people it's under $9 each — less than one 20-minute stand-up per person per month. Bren removes several a week."*

**"We're only 8 people."**
→ Honest fit check. *"Team starts at $299 for up to 25 seats, so it works, but Bren pays off most from ~15 people, when staying informed starts costing real meeting time. If you're 8 today and hiring, start the trial and grow into it."*

**"We're on Asana / Linear."**
→ Honest, roadmap, alternative. *"Not yet — Asana and Linear are on the roadmap. Today Bren reads Jira, Notion, Trello, Airtable, Monday and ClickUp. If your chat and docs are connected you'll still get most of the value; want me to note your interest so the team can follow up?"*

**"Can we try before talking to sales?"**
→ Yes. *"Start the 14-day trial — no card, full Business features. Book a demo only if you want us to map your rollout or you need SSO/SCIM/residency."*
