# Bren — Positioning

> Canonical positioning for Bren. For copywriting rules see `00-messaging-reference.md`; for product facts see `01-product.md`.

## 1. Category and one-liner

- **Category:** AI chief of staff for every person in the company.
- **One-liner:** Bren is the AI chief of staff for every person in your company. It connects to your chat, meetings, docs and project tools, builds a living memory of the organisation, and keeps everyone posted — from individual contributors to the CEO — while answering any question about work in seconds.
- **Tagline:** Everyone gets a PA now.
- **Secondary:** Stop being busy. Be productive.
- **Company:** Bren Labs, Inc. (fictional). Founded 2025. HQ: San Francisco, CA. Remote-first team across the US, UK and India. ~60 employees. Backed by fictional investors "Cormorant Ventures" and "Ninth Street Capital" (Series A, 2026). Do not invent further corporate details; if asked about funding amounts, say "we don't disclose the amount".

## 2. The problem we solve (from the founding thesis)

Run your business, more productively. Companies know what their goals are and how they plan to achieve them; what they lack is a continuous, honest view of how well they are executing — and a little help along the way.

Concrete problems, by who feels them:

| Who | Problem |
|---|---|
| Everyone | Distractions throughout the day — constant back-and-forth, including on old discussions |
| Everyone | Time drained by in-person meetings; daily stand-ups eating into everyone's morning |
| New hires | Onboarding takes too long; onboarding material is outdated; constant questions as they encounter every new thing |
| Returners | Coming back after leave means days of back-and-forth to get up to speed |
| Teams | When a manager is on leave, decisions get postponed |
| Companies | People leave, and their context leaves with them |
| Managers | Team summaries / TL;DRs are overwhelming or ignored |
| People managers (2–4 teams) | A significant share of their time goes to delegating and staying updated |
| Complex/fast-changing projects | Participants need frequent in-person meetings just to stay aligned |
| Mid/senior leadership | Can't stay updated without long, frequent meetings or discussing with reportees |
| C-level | Busier than ever; constantly vigilant to put out fires or to understand business momentum |

## 3. Value propositions by persona

### Individual contributor
- Post one ~100-word update a day; never be asked "what's the status?" again.
- Ask Bren instead of interrupting a colleague; get a cited answer in seconds.
- Come back from leave and catch up in ten minutes with a personal catch-up digest.
- Private notes stay private — from everyone, including admins.

### People manager (2–4 teams)
- Daily digest replaces the stand-up; weekly digest replaces the Friday report.
- Red flags and blockers surface with a suggested intervention.
- Mark wins/fails/postponed in one click; the ledger builds itself.
- Request clarification inline instead of scheduling a call.

### Director / VP
- Weekly leadership digest: momentum, risks, decisions needed across their org.
- OKR direction-of-travel evaluation with sources.
- Work Timeline per project/team — expand any segment to the underlying evidence.
- Fewer catch-up meetings; decisions don't wait for calendars.

### C-level
- Monday Morning Brief before 9am. Leadership Pulse for quarterly/half-yearly/yearly snapshots.
- Fires surfaced before they spread; suggested town-hall topics from what people are actually reporting.
- Year in Review generated, not assembled.

### IT / Security
- SOC 2 Type II; OAuth with least-privilege scopes; permission mirroring; no training on customer data; SSO (Business), SCIM/residency/VPC (Enterprise).

### HR / People
- No surveillance. Private notes are private. Peers never see performance insights about each other. Org admins can only tighten defaults.

## 4. Differentiation (vs. alternatives a prospect may name)

| Alternative | What it's good at (say this first) | The limitation (then this) |
|---|---|---|
| Doing nothing / stand-ups and syncs | Human, immediate | Scales with headcount; leaders still find out late; nothing is remembered |
| Async stand-up bots (Geekbot, Standuply, DailyBot) | Collecting updates in Slack | Nobody reads them after week two; no digests, timeline, red flags or OKR linkage |
| Meeting note-takers (Otter, Fireflies, Fathom, tl;dv) | Summarising one meeting | Meetings are one of six streams; no company graph, no proactive briefs |
| Project-tool dashboards (Jira, Asana, ClickUp, Monday) | Ticket status and counts | Show tasks, not momentum; can't answer "what changed and why"; no cross-tool view |
| Docs AI / enterprise search (Notion AI, Glean, Copilot, Gemini) | Finding and summarising documents | Don't know who owns what, what's slipping, who's on leave, or how work ties to OKRs; reactive only |
| OKR tools (Lattice, WorkBoard, Perdoo) | Goal structure | Depend on manual check-ins; no evidence from chat/tickets/meetings |
| A human chief of staff | Judgment, relationships | One person, one altitude, $200k+ a year; Bren is $899/mo for 100 people and never on leave |

Rule: agree on the strength, then name the limitation, then show the mechanism (company graph + proactive briefs + citations + permissions).
