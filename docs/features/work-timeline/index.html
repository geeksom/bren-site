The problem

The history of a project lives in six tools and three people's heads.

Why did the launch slip? When was the scope cut? Who agreed to the dependency? The answers are in a Slack thread from April, a Jira comment, a meeting nobody recorded, and a Notion page that was edited over. Reconstructing it takes an afternoon — if the people are still here.

How Bren does it

Chronology on the left. Consequences on the right.

One timeline per entity

Open a timeline for a project, a task, a team, an objective or a person (subject to permissions). Updates, status changes, task comments, PRs and meeting snapshots are placed in time.

Wins and failures alongside

Manager-marked outcomes and Bren-detected turning points sit on the right column so the shape of a project is visible at a glance.

"Elaborate this"

Any segment expands to the underlying evidence — the exact messages, tickets, docs and meeting excerpts — or to a longer narrative written by Bren with citations.

Permissions travel with the data

A timeline only shows what you could see in the source tools plus what Bren's Participatory / Public / Selective settings allow.

FAQ

Work Timeline: the details

How far back does it go?

Team: 12 months of searchable history (12-month backfill on connect). Business and Enterprise: unlimited, subject to what the source tools expose.

Can I see a timeline for a person?

Managers can see timelines for direct and indirect reportees. Everyone can see their own. Peers see shared-project contributions only.

Can I export it?

Yes — PDF and JSON/CSV export for any timeline you can view.
